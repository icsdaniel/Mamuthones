# To check with the Mamoiada community

The game depicts a living tradition. Everything below is our best understanding and must be checked with
people from Mamoiada (for example the Museo delle Maschere Mediterranee) before launch. Until then,
player-facing text avoids these details or uses plain words.

## Names and words
- Local names for the mask, the sheepskin, the load of bells, the small front bells, the Issohadores'
  jacket and cap, and the calls. The game only uses "soha" for the rope.
- Spelling of Mamuthone / Mamuthones and Issohadore / Issohadores in Italian and in Sardinian.

## Calendar and route
- The procession's days: first outing at the Sant'Antonio bonfires (16–17 January), then Carnival Sunday
  and Shrove Tuesday. Is the order of the game's seven stops faithful? Is "The Workshop" (the night
  before, preparing masks and bells) appropriate?
- What happens at each moment, so the stop cards say only true things.

## Depiction
- Masks: forms, wood types, finishes; which variations are real and acceptable in a customiser.
- Dress: sheepskins (colours), the kerchief, straps, how the bells are tied and how heavy the load is.
- Issohadores: jacket, mask, cap, bandolier of bells, rope; how the rope throw works and who gets caught.
- Movement: how the rows move and jolt, how many Mamuthones and Issohadores, who leads.

## Sound
- Field recordings of real bells, made or licensed with consent, to replace the synthesized ones.
- Whether original music "in the spirit of" canto a tenore and launeddas is welcome, and whether local
  musicians could record the real thing.

## Items added by the build
- The rope (soha): the in-scene rope should look like the real one, a natural rush or hemp rope (the
  red rope stays only as the swipe note's symbol). Confirm its material and how it's thrown.
- Issohadore costume: shawl or sash, cap shape, white trousers with dark leggings, the bandolier of
  small bells.
- Mamuthone dress: kerchief colour and knot, strap layout, whether small bells are worn on the front,
  how the bell load is arranged on the back, the leggings.
- Settings: the church and bell tower are generic, not Mamoiada's; houses and crowd are generic; where
  the Sant'Antonio bonfire stands; the workshop details (masks on a shelf, hanging bells, sheepskin).
- Mask option names ("grimace", "knotted", "aquiline"...) are descriptive English/Italian, not local
  terms.
- Whether a halo or sunburst behind a mask (the workshop card, the logo) is appropriate.
- Which mask finishes are acceptable (the game keeps them black to dark brown-black).
- Bells: the clapper material, and the number and sizes of bells in one Mamuthone's load (the game uses
  5, 8 or 13 depending on the set).
- The Issohadores' calls: the game's call is invented and uses vowels only. How do they really call?
- The launeddas-style drone used for held notes.
